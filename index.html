Less rice, more milk

The changing Malaysian plate

Do we still grow what we eat?

By Yeoh Sharmanne · 14/10/2026

Rice is still at the centre of many Malaysian meals. But the numbers behind our plates are shifting: rice supplies a smaller slice of our calories, while milk and eggs supply a larger one. Scroll down to see what changed, where our food grows, and why the paddy harvest for each person is shrinking.

Malaysians now get fewer calories from rice and more from milk and eggs than in 2010, while the paddy harvested per person has fallen by a third since 2014.

Rice now supplies fewer than 1 in 4 calories, down from nearly 3 in 10

FAO, the United Nations food agency, estimates how much food energy is available to each person in Malaysia every day. In 2010, rice supplied about 30 of every 100 calories. By 2023 it supplied about 24.

Calories available per person

FAO’s estimate of the food available for people to eat in a country, divided by its population. It is not a measure of what people actually eat.

FAO

The Food and Agriculture Organization of the United Nations.

Since 2010 Malaysians eat 15% less rice and 75% more milk

Comparing the average of 2010–2012 with the average of 2021–2023, the food available per person shows about 15% less rice and about 75% more milk. Eggs are up about 40% and fruit about 34%, while fish is down about 11%. Flour and sugar have hardly changed.

Three-year average

A single year can jump up or down, so we compare the average of three years at the start with three years at the end.

Kilograms per person

The weight of a food available to each person over one year.

Kedah grows 38% of our paddy, and Pahang grows 45% of our vegetables

Four states grew 72% of the 2024 paddy harvest, and Kedah alone grew 38%. Pahang grew 45% of the vegetables, and Johor grew 39% of the fruit.

Hectare and tonne

A hectare is 10,000 square metres (100 metres by 100 metres). A tonne is 1,000 kilograms.

Paddy

Rice as it is harvested, still in its husk, before milling.

We harvest a third less paddy per person than in 2014, but the rice on the plate fell only 3%

Between 2014 and 2023 the paddy harvested for each person in Malaysia fell by about a third, from 93 kilograms to 62 kilograms. Yet the rice available per person fell by only 3%, from 121 kilograms to 118 kilograms.

Index (2014 = 100)

We set the 2014 value to 100 so two different measures can share one scale. A value of 67 means one-third lower than in 2014.

Paddy is not rice

Milling removes the husk, so a tonne of paddy gives less than a tonne of rice. That is why we compare changes, not weights.

Fewer fields and lower yields each cut about 15% off the harvest, and Kedah and Sarawak account for half of the loss

Paddy fields shrank by about 15% between 2014 and 2024, and the harvest from each hectare fell by about 15% too. Together they cut the national harvest by 28%. Kedah and Sarawak account for about half of the tonnes lost.

Yield

Tonnes of paddy harvested from each hectare of field.

Small states, big swings

Johor, Melaka and Negeri Sembilan grow very little paddy, so their yields can change a lot from year to year.

More than half of the crop land DOSM counts grows paddy, so what happens to rice farming matters

Of the nine crop groups in DOSM’s records, paddy takes up 53% of the planted land. Fruit uses about a third of the land that paddy does, yet its harvest weighs almost the same: 1.97 million tonnes against 2.06 million.
